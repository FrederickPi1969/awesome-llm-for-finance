Abstract
Generative AI breakthroughs have reshaped ﬁnancial analytics, introducing
seamless, language-based interactions that expand access t o valuable insights.
Traditionally, ﬁnancial analysis relied on rigid, rule-driven t ools requiring spe-
cialized skills, often yielding 75% accuracy and faltering with complex queries.
DeepFinLLM overcomes these barriers by blending advanced langua ge under-
standing with real-time ﬁnancial data, oﬀering precise respons es with a 94%
accuracy rate and 0.8-second delivery time. Merging artiﬁcial in telligence with
dynamic market data, it provides adaptability and scalabilit y across diverse
ﬁnancial contexts. Users, from expert investors to novices, prais e its intuitive
interface, with 90% valuing its fast, reliable insights. Handli ng structured ﬁnan-
cial information and open-ended inquiries, DeepFinLLM facilita tes deep analysis
of investment options, market trends, and risks, enhanced by rea l-time updates
for relevance in volatile markets. By simplifying complex tasks and encouraging
data-informed decisions, DeepFinLLM transforms ﬁnancial analyt ics to serve a
broader audience eﬀectively.

1 Introduction
The rapid advancement of artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) and NLP has fundament ally
reshaped how machines interpret and respond to complex human queri es. In recent
years, LLMs have showcased exceptional abilities in processing int ricate datasets and
generating contextually relevant insights across various domains, incl uding ﬁnancial
analytics, market forecasting, and investment decision-making. Howev er, delivering
precise, real-time ﬁnancial insights requires eﬀective integrat ion of dynamic market
data and semantic understanding, particularly when handling vast, vol atile ﬁnan-
cial corpora. Traditional ﬁnancial analysis tools, reliant on rule-based algor ithms and
sparse statistical methods, often fail to capture the nuanced dynami cs of market
trends, achieving accuracy rates of only 70% and struggling with multi-d imensional
queries (Adams & Lee,
2022). Similarly, while modern dense retrieval techniques using
vector-based representations enhance semantic matching, they may o verlook critical
real-time updates, reducing their eﬀectiveness in fast-paced ﬁ nancial environments.
To address these shortcomings, hybrid systems that combine the str engths of tra-
ditional rule-based approaches and advanced AI-driven methods have em erged as a
promising solution. By integrating lexical precision with semantic depth and real-time
data, such systems achieve superior accuracy and responsiveness, es sential for deliv-
ering actionable ﬁnancial insights. Additionally, the incorporation of adap tive data
processing techniques has improved contextual relevance by dyn amically aligning live
market feeds with user queries, enhancing both precision and analyt ical quality (Carter
& Nguyen, 2023). Despite these advancements, challenges persist in eﬃciently merging
real-time data with LLM outputs and evaluating the reliability of ﬁnanci al insights,
particularly in volatile market conditions.
In our proposed system, we introduce DeepFinLLM, an intelligent ﬁnan cial advisor
that combines sparse rule-based analysis with dense LLM-driven retr ieval, utilizing a
fusion strategy to balance accuracy and timeliness. The system lever ages the DeepSeek
Chat V3 model for semantic processing and the Financial Modeling Pre p (FMP) API
for real-time data integration, ensuring that retrieved ﬁnancial cont ext is both rele-
vant and current (Patel & Lee, 2022). Furthermore, the generated insights are assessed
using a comprehensive evaluation framework, incorporating metrics s uch as accuracy
(94.2%) for correctness, inference latency (0.8 seconds) for eﬃcienc y, and factual con-
sistency (96.5%) for reliability. This multi-faceted evaluation appr oach provides a
robust assessment of the quality and applicability of the ﬁnancial advic e generated.
One of the primary challenges in ﬁnancial analytics lies in maintaining contex-
tual accuracy and responsiveness across diverse market scenarios. Trad itional tools
often struggle with real-time adaptability, resulting in delayed or imprecise insights.
DeepFinLLM tackles this by employing a hybrid retrieval strategy t hat captures
both structured ﬁnancial data and semantic market trends, ensuring that insights
remain contextually faithful (Chang & Liu, 2024). However, existing ﬁnancial advisory
systems still face several limitations:
• Limited real-time awareness : Many systems rely on static data inputs, leading
to outdated insights or incomplete market representations, which di minish decision-
making quality (Hughes & Wang, 2022).

Financial advisory systems originated with basic tools designed to aggregat e and ana-

6 Threats to Validity
The evaluation of DeepFinLLM, while comprehensive, faces multiple threats to validity
that could shape the interpretation of its performance across varied cont exts. These
threats span internal, external, construct, statistical, ecological, and temporal validity,
each demanding careful examination to ensure reliable conclusions.
• Internal V alidity: Dependence on benchmark datasets like FinQA and Con-
vFinQA introduces an overﬁtting risk, as DeepFinLLM might have been subtly
tailored to these tasks during development. This could inﬂate metr ics such as accu-
racy (94.2%) and nDCG (0.95), potentially overstating real-world generaliz ation.
Moreover, the selection of evaluation queries might skew results to ward DeepFin-
LLM ’s strengths, such as numerical reasoning, while masking weaknesses in rare
ﬁnancial scenarios, suggesting a bias in the assessment process.
• External V alidity: The limited scope of the datasets hampers generalizability, as
they may not encompass all ﬁnancial contexts, including emerging mark ets or niche
assets. While DeepFinLLM performs strongly on standardized tasks (e.g., 92.5% on
FinQA), its capability in unregulated settings or with non-English qu eries remains
unproven, constraining claims of broad applicability, a challenge also highlighted by
(Smith & Taylor,
2024) in real-time analytics research.
• Construct Validity : Metrics like Explainability Score (0.92) and Truthfulness
Score (0.96) rely on subjective human evaluations or automated proxies, wh ich may
not fully capture true interpretability or accuracy. For instance, f actual consistency
(96.5%) assumes ﬂawless ground-truth data, yet ﬁnancial datasets can be noi sy or
outdated, possibly distorting outcomes. Likewise, detecting the Hal lucination Rate
(1.2%) depends on thorough fact-checking, which might miss subtle err ors.
• Statistical V alidity: Small sample sizes in certain tests, such as risk-related querie s
for the Risk Sensitivity Score (0.95), could undermine statistical s tability, poten-
tially exaggerating robustness. Comparisons with baselines like Bloom bergGPT lack
statistical signiﬁcance testing, weakening conﬁdence in superiori ty claims, a concern
echoed by (Chen & Wu, 2025) regarding evaluation precision in AI systems.
• Ecological V alidity: The controlled evaluation environment may not reﬂect real-
world conditions, where users grapple with noisy, incomplete data un der time
constraints. DeepFinLLM ’s success in sterile benchmarks might not fully translate
to practical ﬁnancial decision-making with dynamic, ambiguous inputs .
• T emporal V alidity: Results are tied to a speciﬁc moment (April 2025) and current
market conditions, yet ﬁnancial landscapes shift quickly. Without on going updates
to its training data or API sources, DeepFinLLM ’s eﬀectiveness could wane over
time, an unquantiﬁed risk at present.
Mitigating these threats requires future work to diversify datase ts, explore edge
cases, reﬁne subjective metrics, conduct statistical tests, si mulate real-world usage, and

track temporal changes, ensuring DeepFinLLM ’s reliability across diverse ﬁnancial
scenarios.
